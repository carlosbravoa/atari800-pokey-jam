The music generator in Ballblazer was an algorithmic composition system designed by Peter Langston at Lucasfilm Games.
How Riffology Works
- The Song of the Grid: The iconic theme music, known as "Song of the Grid", played continuously between matches without ever exactly repeating.
- Predefined Riffs: The lead melody used a technique called "riffology," assembling notes from a predefined set of 32 eight-note melody fragments.
- Algorithmic Choices: An algorithm randomly selected and combined these fragments while adjusting parameters like playback speed, loudness, note omission, and rhythmic breaks.
- Accompaniment: Basslines, drums, and chords were similarly generated on the fly using a simplified version of the same random assembly approach.
- POKEY Sound Chip: The music relied on the unique capabilities of the POKEY sound hardware found in Atari computers and specialized cartridges.

The Mechanics of "Riffology"

The actual algorithmic system ran inside the game's assembly code, executing at the start of every video frame (50 or 60 times a second). Instead of storing a long, memory-heavy track file, the game stored a compressed database of musical ideas.ComponentHow the Atari Handled ItThe Lead MelodyThe 6502 assembly code kept an array of 32 distinct "riffs." Each riff was a micro-sequence of exactly 8 notes. Each note was stored as a single byte corresponding to a verified "in-tune" frequency value for the POKEY register.The "Riff Picker"The algorithm used the POKEY's built-in hardware random number generator (which continuously cycled an internal polynomial counter) to roll a virtual dice. This selected which 8-note fragment to queue next.Live Parametric AlterationsWhile a riff was playing, the engine didn't just read the bytes straight. It mathematically altered them on the fly: • Note Omission: Randomly skipping a note and extending the previous one.• Rhythmic Breaks: Dynamically inserting rests instead of playing a note.• Speed Modifications: Forcing a riff to play at double-time or half-time.The Accompaniment LayersWhile one POKEY channel handled the erratic lead melody, the remaining three channels ran simplified versions of the algorithm to generate the steady basslines, chord backdrops, and synthetic percussion noises.By dynamically feeding these algorithmically calculated numbers directly into the POKEY chip's hardware registers ($D200 to $D20F in memory), the Atari transformed basic math into an infinite, evolving jazz session.
